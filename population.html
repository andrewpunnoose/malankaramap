Received Through Marriage

These figures are overwhelmingly marriages of one kind: an Orthodox man takes a wife from another denomination, and it is she who converts into the Church, rather than the reverse. Marthoma Protestants make up by far the largest share of these converts, not by chance but by geography — the districts where Orthodox Christians are most concentrated, namely Pathanamthitta, Alappuzha, and Kollam, are the same southern districts where Marthoma Protestants are also predominant, the two communities having lived side by side since their nineteenth-century split. Malankara Orthodox settlement in Kerala has traditionally been described as running from the village of Chathannoor in the south to Chalissery in the north — that is, from Kollam district up through Palakkad. Conversions arising from marriage to communities outside this list — such as Pentecostal churches — are negligible: in nearly every such intermarriage, it is the Orthodox spouse who converts to Pentecostalism, rather than the reverse.

A Note on These Figures

These are Malankara Map's own compiled estimates for 2022, drawn from parish and diocesan reporting across Kerala's fourteen districts and the Church's worldwide dioceses. District population percentages are calculated against each district's total population. As with any population estimate of a large, dispersed community, exact figures shift year to year; this page is updated as better data becomes available.
