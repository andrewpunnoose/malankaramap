Saints of Malankara

Nineteen centuries after the Apostle Thomas first set foot on this coast, his flame still passes from hand to hand. It has burned, in every generation, in men who fasted in forest hermitages and men who stood in courtrooms; in bishops who crossed the sea to Jerusalem and in priests who never left their own parish gate; in the learned and the unlettered alike. The Church calls this company her cloud of witnesses — not because their lives were without struggle, but because their struggle was carried, start to finish, toward God.

Gathered here are eleven of them. Four have been formally glorified by the Episcopal Synod — their sainthood a settled matter of the Church's calendar and canon. The remaining seven are held in a gentler, older kind of honour: the veneration of the parishes and families who still keep vigil at their tombs, whose causes for canonisation are not closed but simply not yet finished — the Church, unlike time, is never in a hurry.

Their bodies rest in the soil of Malankara. Their intercession, the faithful hold, rests nowhere in particular — it is simply always near.

Titles below follow the honour each has so far received from the Church: Saint (canonised), Venerable, and Blessed. A gold ribbon marks any saint whose feast falls on today's date.

15 June 1848 – 2 November 1902
Saint

Gregorios of Parumala

Popularly Parumala Thirumeni
ഗീവർഗീസ് മാർ ഗ്രീഗോറിയോസ്
Metropolitan of Niranam Entombed at St. Peter & St. Paul's Church, Parumala First declared saint of Malankara

Born on 15 June 1848 to Kochu Mathai and Mariam of the Pallathetta family at Mulanthuruthy, the boy called Kochaippora was given the baptismal name Geevarghese. His mother died when he was two, and it was his eldest sister Mariam who raised him thereafter. At ten, already known for his devotion and his ear for the old Syriac chants, he was made a reader-deacon; the Malpan who trained him died of smallpox, and the boy himself survived the same disease only by what his family took to be providence.

He withdrew for a time to the Vettickal Dayara, living a hermit's rhythm of fasting and prayer, and was made a Ramban there in 1872 by the Malankara Metropolitan Pulikkottil Joseph Mar Dionysius. Three years later he served as secretary and translator to the visiting Patriarch of Antioch, Peter III, and assisted at the historic Synod of Mulanthuruthy in 1876 — the same year in which, at the age of twenty-eight, he himself was consecrated a bishop, the youngest of six raised together, and given the see of Niranam under the new name Geevarghese Mar Gregorios. His brother bishops, out of affection for his youth, called him Kochu Thirumeni — the Young Bishop.

He made his seat at Parumala, in a small riverside building called the Azhippura, and there gave himself to a threefold labour that would define his episcopate: the government of his diocese, the formation of the young deacons who gathered around him for training, and a restless missionary energy that reached the poor and the excluded alike. Schools rose at his instigation across Malankara — at Kunnamkulam, Mulanthuruthy, Niranam, Thumpamon, Thiruvalla — open, by his express wish, regardless of religion or gender. In 1895 he made pilgrimage to Jerusalem and returned to publish Malayalam's first printed travelogue, an account of the journey later brought into English a century on.

Among the deacons who trained under him at Parumala were three who would themselves shape the Church for decades after: Vattasseril Geevarghese, who became Dionysius VI; Kuttikattu Paulose, later Mar Athanasios of Aluva; and Kallasseril Geevarghese, who would be raised as Catholicos Baselios Geevarghese II. A chronic illness overtook him in his last years, and on 2 November 1902 he died at Parumala, mourned by a gathering the parish had never seen the like of before. Within decades his tomb had become the foremost pilgrimage site of the Malankara Church, and the testimonies of the faithful to his intercession never ceased. On the 45th anniversary of his death, 2 November 1947, Catholicos Baselios Geevarghese II — once his own deacon — declared him a saint of the Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church. Forty years later, on 20 October 1987, His Holiness Ignatius Zakka I, Patriarch of Antioch, solemnly confirmed his sainthood for the wider Syriac Orthodox communion.

“…that the land would yield a hundred, sixty, and thirtyfold harvest.”

Remembered blessing over the fields near Mazhuvannoor
Commemorated Today
29 April 1836 – 23 September 1923
Saint

Alvares Julius

Metropolitan Alvares Mar Yulios of Goa, Ceylon & India outside Kerala
അൽവാറീസ് മാർ യൂലിയോസ്
Founder, Brahmavar Mission Entombed at St. Mary's Orthodox Church, Ribandar Declared regional saint by Cath. Paulose II

Antonio Francisco Xavier Alvares was born on 29 April 1836 into an aristocratic Roman Catholic family in Portuguese Goa. Ordained to the Roman priesthood in 1869, he spent eighteen years serving his flock there, becoming known equally for his philanthropy toward Goa's poor and for a patriotism that would not sit quietly under foreign rule. That same conviction eventually made his position in Goa untenable, and in 1887 he left for Brahmavar, in what is now Karnataka, so as to shepherd his growing following without interference from the colonial authorities.

In Brahmavar he was received into the Syriac Orthodox Church by Mar Gregorios of Parumala, who raised him to the rank of Ramban. Alvares organised his disciples into an Orthodox mission that nonetheless retained the Roman liturgical rite — making his Brahmavar community one of the earliest Western Orthodox communities to return to communion with the East. On 29 July 1889 he was consecrated Metropolitan Alvares Mar Yulios of Goa, Ceylon, and India outside Kerala, by Mar Gregorios and Malankara Metropolitan Pulikkottil Mar Joseph Dionysius II, and the mission he led went on to spread through Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, and Maharashtra, eventually serving some five thousand families across dozens of parishes.

In 1892 he took part in the consecration of the French Old Catholic priest Joseph Rene Vilatte as Metropolitan Timotheos, sent onward to reconcile Old Catholic communities in America to Orthodoxy. Returning to Goa in 1913, Alvares faced sustained persecution at the hands of the Portuguese authorities on account of his Orthodoxy: he was falsely accused, arrested, stripped of his episcopal vestments in the open street, and held in a filthy lockup before being beaten and brought before the courts. He bore it all and continued his work among Goa's poor and its lepers until his death in Panaji on 23 September 1923 — denied, by the same authorities, even an Orthodox funeral.

He was buried quietly in the municipal cemetery of St. Ines. His tomb went unmarked and largely forgotten until its rediscovery in January 1967 by Metropolitan Mathews Mar Athanasios; his relics were translated to St. Mary's Orthodox Church, Ribandar, in 1979, and moved to their present tomb in 2001. On 6 December 2015, Catholicos Baselios Paulose II declared Alvares Mar Julius a regional saint of the Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church, together with his closest collaborator in Brahmavar, Fr. Roque Zephrin Noronha.

Commemorated Today
31 October 1858 – 23 February 1934
Saint

Dionysius the Illuminator

Popularly Vattasseril Thirumeni · “Malankara Sabha Bhasuran”
വട്ടശ്ശേരിൽ മാർ ദിവന്നാസ്യോസ്
15th Malankara Metropolitan Entombed at the Old Seminary, Kottayam Restorer of the Catholicate

Geevarghese Vattasseril was born on 31 October 1858, the fifth child of Joseph Vattasseril of Mallappally and Aleyamma Kolathu Kalathil of Kurichy. While still a high-school student at CMS Kottayam, he was ordained a sub-deacon by the visiting Patriarch of Antioch, Peter III. He went on to study for four years at the Old Seminary and, as secretary to Mar Gregorios of Parumala, absorbed under that saint's own tutelage a scholarship in Syriac and theology so thorough that by 1880 the Church had named him Malankara Malpan — a Doctor of the Church. He wrote prolifically: a Syriac primer, an edition of the Church's order of worship, a Malayalam translation of the Holy Service book, and Mathopadeshasarangal, still counted among the most authoritative expositions of Orthodox doctrine in the language. He also served for eight years as Principal of the M.D. Seminary School in Kottayam.

Ordained Ramban in 1903 and consecrated Metropolitan at the Sehion House in Jerusalem — the traditional site of the Last Supper — on 31 May 1908, he took the name Geevarghese Dionysius and, the following year, succeeded to the full office of Malankara Metropolitan. It was in this office that his defining conflict began. The Patriarch of Antioch, Abdulla, pressed every bishop of that generation to sign over deeds acknowledging his authority not only in spiritual matters but in the Church's temporal administration — her finances, her schools, her parishes — a claim the Travancore courts had already rejected in 1889. Every other bishop of the period yielded. Dionysius alone refused, and for that refusal was excommunicated. The Church's Managing Committee stood by him regardless, and in 1911 the Senior Patriarch, Mar Abdul Messiah — recognised by Malankara as canonical — declared the excommunication null and void.

More than once in the years of open conflict that followed, tradition preserved in the Church holds that agents of the Patriarchal faction made attempts on his life — attempts from which he emerged each time unharmed, a providence his people came to read as a further sign of the hand of God upon him. In 1912, at the invitation of Dionysius and his supporters, Mar Abdul Messiah travelled to Kerala and installed Paulose Mar Ivanios as the first Catholicos of the East in nine centuries, restoring to the Malankara Church the autocephaly it had not held since the Catholicate's ancient seat at Seleucia-Ctesiphon. It is in no small part because Dionysius refused to bend that the Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church remains self-governing to this day.

He drafted, in his final years and with the Patriarch Ignatius Abdul Masih II's assistance, the first working text of a Church constitution — the basis for the Constitution formally adopted in 1934, the year of his death. Struck by paralysis on 16 February 1934, he received the Church's last rites from Baselios Geevarghese II five days before he died, on 23 February, and was buried in the chapel of the Old Seminary, Kottayam, beside his predecessors. The words later placed on his tomb — "the time will not dim his glory" — proved prophetic: on 24 February 2003, the 69th anniversary of his death, the Episcopal Synod canonised him as the second Indian saint of the Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church, after Gregorios of Parumala.

“…the responsibility to upkeep that institution which most of you along with myself truly believe to be indispensable for the survival, prosperity and progress of our Church — namely, the Catholicate.”

From his final encyclical to the Malankara Church

Saint

Roque Zephrin Noronha

Popularly Hodlo Padreab — “the Great Father”
റോക് സെഫ്രിൻ നൊറോൺഹ
First Vicar General, St. Mary's Cathedral, Brahmavar Entombed at St. Mary's Orthodox Cathedral, Brahmavar Declared regional saint by Cath. Paulose II

Born at Angediva in Bardez, Goa, on 20 October 1850, Roque Zephrin Noronha trained at the Rachol Royal Seminary and was ordained a Roman Catholic priest in 1881, going on to serve as assistant vicar of Salvador de Monte Church and later as Vicar of Panaji's Ribandar Church. Like Antonio Alvares, whose cause he had already joined, Noronha grew increasingly troubled by Portuguese rule over the Church in Goa, and it was through this shared conviction that the two men became lifelong collaborators.

When Alvares was consecrated Metropolitan in 1889, he entrusted the newly founded St. Mary's Orthodox Syrian Cathedral at Brahmavar — then still a thatched-roof building — to Noronha as its first Vicar General. Noronha celebrated the community's first Holy Qurbana there on Resurrection Sunday, 1889, and went on to serve the Konkani Orthodox community for nearly fifty years, founding St. Mary's Orthodox Syrian Church at Kandlur around 1905 and St. Antony's Church at Kolalgiri in 1923. In 1916 he founded the Cosmopolitan Higher Primary School, Brahmavar's first institution of basic education, naming it deliberately for the inclusivity he believed in.

He was, beyond his priestly office, a physician of sorts — a self-taught practitioner of indigenous medicine who treated anyone who came to him, without payment and without regard to caste or creed, a generosity that earned him the town's enduring affection and the title Hodlo Padreab, the Great Father. He also found time to write eight books defending and explaining the Orthodox faith, five of which survive. In his final years a mark in the shape of a cross was said to have appeared on his back; not long after, weakened by diabetes, he died on 23 July 1936 at the age of eighty-six, and was buried at St. Mary's Orthodox Cathedral, Brahmavar, where the faithful — Christian and otherwise — continued to seek his intercession for generations. On 6 December 2015, Catholicos Baselios Paulose II declared him a regional saint of the Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church, alongside Alvares Mar Julius.

Venerable

Younan of Anchal

Popularly Anchal Achan
യൗനാൻ കത്തനാർ
Priest of Kottarakkara Church Entombed at Kottarakkara Church (now Mar Thoma) Feast: Karkidakam 22

Almost nothing certain is recorded of Younan Kathanar's early years — only that he was born, by historical estimate, sometime around 1770–80, into the Marunthalizhikathu family of Anchal, and that within his own lifetime his neighbours had already begun to call him a saint. He began his priesthood walking the sixteen kilometres between Anchal and Kottarakkara Church on foot, before at last moving into the church compound itself, where he lived out the rest of his days.

His asceticism was of the plainest, most self-effacing kind. One morning, returning from his ritual bath, he found his elder brother's wife sweeping the room he lived in. He did not enter. Standing at the threshold, he asked only that his Bible and his books be handed out to him, and with them he left for Kottarakkara and did not return — a small, wordless renunciation that the parish never forgot.

Before his death he told the people of Kottarakkara something stranger still: that they should open his grave on the forty-first day after his passing, and that they would find his body whole. He died, by tradition, on the day of Karkidakam 22 in the Malayalam calendar. On the appointed day the parish did as he had asked, and found the body largely incorrupt — only the tip of the nose and the ends of two fingers showing decay, which the faithful attributed to his having once plucked a rose before the Divine Liturgy. In the long litigation that later divided Malankara's parishes, Kottarakkara Church itself passed to the Mar Thoma Church, and Anchal Achan's tomb remains there today, within its altar. His memory is kept especially at St. George Orthodox Valiya Pally, Anchal, which the Catholicos of the East Baselios Thoma Didymos I later raised to the status of a Valiya Pally — a Great Parish — and a pilgrim centre in his honour.

Philippose of Kayamkulam

Popularly Kayamkulam Rambachan
ഫീലിപ്പോസ് റമ്പാൻ
Translator of the first Malayalam Bible Entombed at St. Thomas Orthodox Cathedral, Kannamcode

Philipose Ramban lived at Mananganazhikathu, his family home in Kayamkulam — a house still remembered as Mananganazhikathu Rambachan's House. He came to his life's great labour almost by invitation: in the early nineteenth century the Scottish chaplain Rev. Claudius Buchanan, travelling through Kerala at a time when Syriac alone served as the Church's liturgical language, urged the Malankara clergy to render the Scriptures into Malayalam for the first time, and placed into Philipose Ramban's hands a Syriac Bible to work from.

Ramban undertook the Four Gospels. In 1811 his translation was completed and printed — lithographed at a press in Bombay under the title Visudha Veda Pustakam — becoming, in that single volume, the first Bible ever to appear in the Malayalam language. It came to be known simply as the Ramban Bible, the ground every later Malayalam translation, from Benjamin Bailey's to Hermann Gundert's, would in some sense stand upon. He died that same year, and was laid to rest at what is now the St. Thomas Orthodox Cathedral, Kannamcode, in Adoor. Every year on Thulam 26, the Church keeps his memory with a Shraadha Perunnal — a memorial feast for the priest who first gave Malankara her Scriptures in her own tongue.

Blessed

Philoxenus of Puthencavu

Popularly Puthencavil Kochu Thirumeni
ഗീവർഗീസ് മാർ ഫിലക്സിനോസ്
Metropolitan of Thumpamon, 1930–1951 Entombed at St. Mary's Orthodox Cathedral, Puthencavu

K.T. Geevarghese was born on 10 June 1897 at Puthencavu on the banks of the Pamba, the second son of Thoma Kathanar of the Kizhakethallekal family and Rachelamma of the Chungathil family. He earned his Bachelor of Divinity at Serampore College — alongside a fellow student named P.T. Geevarghese, who would later become Mar Ivanios of the rival Catholic communion — and an M.A. in English from Calcutta before returning to teach at the M.D. Seminary School.

Ordained deacon and priest in 1929 by his mentor, St. Dionysius of Vattasseril, he was consecrated bishop the following year at Parumala Seminary, taking the name Geevarghese Mar Philoxenos and, as the youngest bishop of his generation, the affectionate title Kochu Thirumeni. He governed the Thumpamon diocese for two decades, and in that time founded or established a striking range of institutions: Catholicate High School and a Teachers' Training College at Pathanamthitta, a Metropolitan High School at his native Puthencavu, Othera Dayara and the Bethel Aramana that became the headquarters of Chengannur diocese, a theological college at Makkamkunnu, and a hospital at Eraviperoor. Alongside Dionysius VI, he was instrumental in persuading a young Baselios Marthoma Mathews I toward theological study, and he led the Church's legal efforts to recover schools ceded to Mar Ivanios's rival mission while Dionysius VI himself was away in Mardin.

He died on 17 April 1951 at the age of fifty-three, at the Marathamcode Mar Gregorios Chapel, where the room and bed he used are still kept as relics of his life. He was buried at St. Mary's Orthodox Cathedral, Puthencavu, near the tombs of Mar Thoma VI and Mar Thoma VIII, and remains a site of pilgrimage for the faithful of Malankara. In 2013, on his sixty-second commemoration, the Catholicate conferred upon him its title of Ratna Deepam.

“His Presence was Joyful, His Voice Sweet, His Words Inspiring, His Manners Endearing, His Smile Unforgettable.”

Inscribed on his tomb

Gregorios of Pampady

Popularly Pampady Thirumeni
കുര്യാക്കോസ് മാർ ഗ്രീഗോറിയോസ്
Metropolitan of Kottayam, 1929–1965 Entombed at St. Kuriakose Monastery, Pothenpuram

Kuriakose was born on a Sunday, 5 April 1885, in the village of Pezhamattam near Pampady, the fifth of ten children of Chacko and Ilichi of the Karingamattom family — a devout household that weathered real hardship. Even as a boy he crossed the caste lines his village otherwise observed without a thought, regularly carrying coconuts and sweets to school for underprivileged classmates the social custom of the day held at arm's length.

Ordained sub-deacon at fourteen and trained at the Old Seminary under Vattasseril Geevarghese Malpan — the future Dionysius VI — he was ordained priest and, the following day, Ramban in 1906, and threw himself into rebuilding the Pampady parish church, largely through the free labour of villagers he organised himself. In 1914 he withdrew to a mud hovel on the wild, animal-haunted hills of Pothenpuram to live as a hermit — a hill he himself took to calling Mount Tabor — and that solitary discipline grew in time into St. Kuriakose Monastery, its foundation stone laid in 1920 and its church completed three years later. He later served at Parumala Seminary, teaching Syriac to the deacons there and personally nursing the ailing Catholicos Mar Baselios Paulose I, who came to call him simply "My Ramban."

On 16 February 1929, the day before his own succession, Catholicos Baselios Geevarghese II consecrated him Metropolitan under the name Kuriakose Mar Gregorios — the last bishop that Catholicos would personally ordain. As Metropolitan of Kottayam for thirty-six years, and as trustee of the Catholicate Endowment from its founding in 1934, he became known above all for a life of continuous prayer credited with visible wonders: the sparing of plague-stricken Kunnamkulam, and rain that broke a punishing drought in Pampady after three days of communal fasting. He treated every visitor, rich or poor, with the same unhurried welcome, and the poet C.P. Chandy immortalised his gentleness toward all living things in a poem titled "The Golden Rose of Pampady." He died at St. Kuriakose Monastery on 5 April 1965, on his own eightieth birthday, and was entombed there on the hill he had once gone to pray alone and called his own Mount Tabor.

“Do good to man — he may turn back to hurt you. Do good to an animal; it will not bite you back. How much worse than a dog are some men?”

Attributed teaching, remembered by his devotees

Mathews of Mylapra

Popularly Mylapra Rambachan
മാത്യൂസ് റമ്പാച്ചൻ
Entombed at Mylapra St. Kuriakose Monastery

Deacon Mathews came of age in the direct company of two of Malankara's great spiritual fathers: Pampady Thirumeni, under whose hand he was ordained full deacon at Pampady Dayara, and Thoma Mar Dionysius, founder of the Mount Tabor Dayara, whom he accompanied on visits across the Niranam diocese. In both men he found a model of administration joined to prayer that he set himself, from a young age, to imitate; while staying with Mar Dionysius at Puthiyacavu church in Mavelikkara, his sermons were already moving parishioners to tears.

Church memory preserves one episode from these years with particular tenderness: a young woman, drawn by his preaching, came to his door hoping he might share his life with her. Deacon Mathews declined the packet of sweets she offered and, gently but firmly, told her that both of them had already resolved on a life given wholly to God, and that this new temptation could only be the work of the adversary trying to unmake two vows at once. He closed his door and returned to prayer. When false allegations later delayed his ordination and Baselios Geevarghese II himself hesitated to advance him, Deacon Mathews accepted the setback without complaint, resolved to remain a deacon for as long as the Church required it of him. A month later, meeting him again at Pathanamthitta and finding him grown a beard, the Catholicos looked at him and simply said the Spirit was calling him to the priesthood after all. He was ordained on 8 May 1932 at Pathikkara Church, Mallappally, and is remembered today at the monastery bearing his name in Mylapra, where he is entombed.

9 August 1924 – 9 December 2012

Barnabas of America

Mathews Mar Barnabas
മാത്യൂസ് മാർ ബർന്നബാസ്
Metropolitan of Idukki & the American Diocese Entombed at St. Peter & St. Paul's Church, Valayanchirangara

Born a twin to Kuruvilla and Mariamma of the Kidangethu Thompra family at Valayanchirangara, the boy called Mathukutty came from a household already marked by the priesthood — both his grandfather and his uncle had served as Orthodox priests before him. He resolved to become a monk at the age of seven, took up a vegetarian discipline he kept for the rest of his life, and spent his school vacations at the Gathsemon Dayara in Piramadam under his uncle Fr. K.P. Paulose, absorbing monastic life at an age when most children were still at play.

His formal education ran an unusually wide course — a degree in Biology from Madras Christian College, a Master's in Botany from Osmania University while serving the Secunderabad parish, and eventually a B.D. from Serampore University by way of Bishop's College, Calcutta — alongside a theological formation begun under his uncle and continued at the Orthodox Theological Seminary in Kottayam. Ordained deacon in 1943 by Baselios Geevarghese II at his own home parish and priest in 1951 by Augen Mar Thimotheos, he spent decades in the Church's service as a teacher, seminary warden, and hospital chaplain before the Malankara Association elected him to the episcopate in 1977.

Consecrated Mathews Mar Barnabas in 1978, he served as assistant bishop of the Angamaly and Kottayam dioceses, then as the founding Metropolitan of the newly formed Idukki Diocese from 1982, and from 1992 as Metropolitan of the undivided American Diocese, later divided into its northern and southern sees. He retired from administrative duties in 2011 and withdrew to the Monastery of St. Kuriakose the Martyr, where he spent his final months. He died on 9 December 2012 and was buried at his home parish, St. Peter and St. Paul's, Valayanchirangara — the same church where, as a boy of seven, he had first resolved to give his life to God.

Ivanios of Kottayam

Geevarghese Mar Ivanios
ഗീവർഗീസ് മാർ ഈവാനിയോസ്
Metropolitan of Kottayam, 1985–2013 Entombed at Mar Baselios Dayara, Njaliyakuzhy Theologian & writer

George was born on 14 November 1940 in Madurai to Mar Thoma parents, K. George and Annamma, and the family moved to Kollam when he was four. It was in his college years that he grew close to the Malankara Orthodox Church, drawn by a friendship with Metropolitan Mathews Mar Coorilos of Kollam — later Baselios Marthoma Mathews II — that would shape the rest of his life. At twenty-one he received chrismation into the Orthodox Church, and two years later was ordained deacon by the same Mar Coorilos, before leaving for England to live among the Cowley Fathers and complete an M.A. in Christian Theology at Oxford.

He returned to Kerala in 1971 to teach Hebrew, Syriac, and Greek at the Orthodox Theological Seminary, Kottayam, at the invitation of Philipose Mar Theophilos, and was ordained priest there in 1973. A cancer diagnosis in 1978 was met and overcome at the Adyar Cancer Institute in Chennai; the following year he was ordained Ramban by Baselios Marthoma Mathews I. Elected to the episcopate by the Malankara Association in 1982, he was consecrated Geevarghese Mar Ivanios in 1985 and appointed Metropolitan of the Kottayam Diocese, an office he held for the rest of his life.

He was, above all, a theologian and a contemplative, remembered for slender, luminous Malayalam works on the interior life — among them Mounathinte Lavanyam ("The Beauty of Silence"), Nirlepam ("Untaintedness"), and Hridayashuddhikaranam ("Purification of the Heart") — writing that continues to be read, and has since been translated into English. He died on 12 April 2013; his funeral, led by Baselios Marthoma Paulose II, was held the following day at Mar Baselios Dayara, Njaliyakuzhy, where he was laid to rest.
