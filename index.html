Nineteen Centuries,
One Continuous Thread

Tradition holds that the Apostle Thomas landed upon the Malabar coast in 52 CE, there founding the earliest communities of worship in the region. Across centuries of contact with the Syrian churches, and later under colonial pressure, that lineage remained unbroken.

In 1912, the Catholicate was re-established in India, affirming anew the Church's apostolic autonomy. Every marker upon the map above carries within it a thread of that same continuous narrative.

A Few Clarifications

Churches claimed by more than one diocese carry more than one pin on the edifice.

Proprietary churches — typically owned by a family or clan association — are consecrated under Malankara Orthodox rites and operate outside diocesan administration.

Since the 1974 schism, parishes of the Knanaya Diocese fall under the direct jurisdiction of the Catholicos in Devalokam.

This map does not count digital congregations, such as the Good Shepherd Orthodox Family Fellowship.
