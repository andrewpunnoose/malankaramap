Titular See of the Knanaya

A titular see under the Catholicos, the Knanaya Diocese preserves the heritage of the endogamous Knanaya community, whose descendants continue to serve and worship in other Orthodox dioceses.

History

The Knanaya tradition holds that in the year 345 a Middle Eastern merchant, Thomas of Cana, led seventy-two Jewish-Christian families from Edessa (modern Urfa) to the coast of Malankara, present-day Kerala. Welcomed by the ruler Cheraman Perumal, this endogamous community settled at Kodungalloor with its own priests, deacons and a leader, Uraha Mar Ouseph (Bishop Joseph of Urfa), and preserved its distinctive cultural, linguistic and West Syriac liturgical traditions over many centuries.

To provide distinct pastoral administration that respected this heritage, the modern ecclesiastical entity was established in 1910. Nine initial parishes were organised by the Knanaya Committee, and Fr. Geevarghese Edavazhickal (Mar Severios) was ordained the first Knanaya Metropolitan.

After the second schism in the Church in 1974, the diocese was placed under the direct control of the Catholicos and now functions as a titular see, since no Knanaya clergy or churches affiliated with it affirm the 1934 Constitution. The diocese under schismatic control has its headquarters at St. Aphrem Seminary, Chingavanam, Kottayam. Nevertheless, Knanaya priests serve in other dioceses, and thousands of Knanaya-descendant Malankara Orthodox faithful, chiefly in places without Knanaya churches, are found mainly in the dioceses of Kottayam and Mavelikara.
