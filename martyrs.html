In Remembrance

Martyrs of Malankara

Martyrs are those who gave their lives for Christ. Since ancient times we have studied the history and stories of the martyrs. Behind the Malankara Church’s history of self-governance and independence lies a history that can never be forgotten: the struggles and suffering of courageous believers.

The Nasrani community grew up through give-and-take with others. The Roman and Syrian camps dangled the bait of “faith-obedience” before it and tried to bind it with the shackles of slavery. The Malankara Nasranis took refuge only in the way and faith of Mar Thoma, who was born in Christ, and they fought and won. Their heroic history remains relevant today.

It is clear that this history cannot be rewritten with honorific titles received from the valleys of Lebanon in the night hours. Society will soon see that the praise-songs and positions of the present day are merely “monkey-and-donkey” mutual flattery (markata-gardabha nyayam). The reason is that the blood of faithful believers, sacrificed like Christ for Mar Thoma’s way in Malankara, stands for this Church.

In the name of standing with the Holy Church, and of refusing to surrender the Church’s independence and positions, many martyrs had to be killed in Church history, both deliberately and unplanned. Those who were attacked and isolated along with them are not few. In one form or another, these attacks continue even today.

Ponodathu Mathai Kathanar, Chalil Korah, Varkey Aashan (Aanapappy), Kadamattom Onankunju, Thottappattu Kuriakose, Malankara Varghese and others died as martyrs at the hands of the breakaway faction.

We do not forget that there are also unknown ones among them.

A candle has been lit in memory of the martyrs.

Martyred 31 March 1912 (Meenam 18, 1087)

Varkey Aashan (Aanapappy)

വർക്കി ആശാൻ (ആനപാപ്പി)
Peringara, near ThiruvallaTeacher & chief bodyguard to St. Dionysius of Vattasseril

The life of Varkey Varghese, the bodyguard of St. Dionysius of Vattasseril, the shining light of the Malankara Church, always pains our hearts. He was the only son of Varkey Aashan of the Panickerveettil family near Karakkal St. George Church in Peringara, near Thiruvalla, and of Mariamma of the Karakkal Chakkumveettil family. He was known as “Aanapappy” (aana means elephant). Out of love for the Church, and inspired by eminent figures such as Mr. K. M. Mammen Mappila, Mr. K. C. Eapen and Rev. P. T. Geevarghese Kathanar, he was appointed chief of the bodyguards of St. Dionysius of Vattasseril, who led the Church through its crises.

Aanapappy was well educated, an expert in martial arts, a teacher, a lover of the Church and an extraordinary wrestler. The killers, and the Patriarchal partisans who gave them the contract, were governed by one thought: if Varkey Varghese were not killed, St. Dionysius of Vattasseril could not be eliminated. The result was that Aanapappy, only 35, was cruelly killed in a conspiracy. No one disputes that he was killed on Meenam 18, 1087 (March 31, 1912), and that the next day was a Monday. Many historians have tried to find his birth date and failed. As his name suggested, he was physically a giant. He faced death threats.

Aanapappy studied up to matriculation (SSLC) and began working life as a teacher, like his father and grandfather. He taught at a government LP school near Kallooppara. His family was prosperous, so his finances were good, and his lifestyle reflected the standing of the time. His sister Annamma married Kochukunju of the famous Inchakkalody family of Chengannur. Aanapappy himself married Annamma of the Attupuram family in Peringara. When his wife’s relatives prepared to sell the Attupuram property, Aanapappy sold his own Karakkal land rather than let others have it, bought that property and settled there. That is how he came to live at Attupuram near Peringara.

History records that he received an order granting him a Ranger post in the Travancore Government’s Excise Department, but did not join the service and went to Kottayam instead.

The background

St. Dionysius of Vattasseril felt that handing the Malankara Church’s ownership and independence to Patriarch Abdulla would amount to betraying the Church. A strong section of the Church opposed Abdulla’s methods of acquiring wealth and the activities that created laxity in the Church. Another section prepared to back the Patriarch. The Church split in two, and the factions reached the height of rivalry.

The Old Seminary in Kottayam was the seat of the Malankara Metropolitan, and the centre of the Church as well as its chief seminary. Its possession lay with three trustees. The clerical and lay trustees opposed to the Metropolitan found a way to take the seminary entirely into their hands: suspending St. Dionysius of Vattasseril. Konat Mathen Malpan and Akkara C. J. Kurien dreamed that if the Patriarch suspended him, the seminary would belong solely to them, and the Patriarch eagerly carried out the suspension as they hinted.

Police proceedings before the Executive Magistrate were underway over who should control the seminary. The suspension was an easy route to seizing the property in that summary case. After the Patriarch’s decree arrived, the opposing faction in the case asked the clerical and lay trustees to remove St. Dionysius of Vattasseril. But in 1912 the Kottayam Division First Class Magistrate ruled by confirming the Metropolitan’s own possession. The trustees appealed to the High Court, which ruled that the seminary was in the joint possession of the three trustees. After that verdict, attempts to seize the seminary and violence broke out. The Kottayam-based lay trustee sought help from prominent local goondas, and efforts began to finish off St. Dionysius of Vattasseril. Bodyguards became essential for the Malankara Metropolitan, and Aanapappy, Varkey Aashan, was appointed as their chief.

Reports reached the Church that Patriarchal partisans were plotting in many ways against St. Dionysius of Vattasseril. Many people from southern parishes such as Thiruvalla, Niranam and Mavelikkara gathered at the Old Seminary, and guards were appointed so that no harm would come to him. Aanapappy was one of those appointed from Thiruvalla. K. M. Mammen Mappila (Kochu Mammen Vakil) sent him, as he stated in his testimony in the Vattippanam case. The one who led this effort was K. C. Eapen, known as “Kerala Deenabandhu.” As Aanapappy left for Kottayam, K. C. Eapen, the brother of K. C. Mammen Mappila, gave his approval by tying a kasavu kavani around his neck and wrapping a ponnada (honorary shawl) around him.

In a 1992 letter sent to Adv. P. C. Mathew, Divyasree Manalil Yakob Kathanar, secretary to St. Dionysius of Vattasseril, wrote:

“When the opposing faction created trouble in the seminary and some Muslim rowdies began abusing the Metropolitan and harassing him, it was the Thiruvalla leaders who brought Aanapappy to protect him.”

Manalil Yakob Kathanar, letter to Adv. P. C. Mathew, 1992

The murder

Aanapappy’s killers aimed to scare, seize and beat the guards. The guards of St. Dionysius of Vattasseril would be isolated, and then the seminary captured. Some godless people, with muscle, money and good connections in authority, held a secret conspiracy. Plainly stated, on Meenam 18, 1087 (March 31, 1912), at about 6 pm, on the sand-bank of the Meenachil river north of the Old Seminary near Govindapuram in Kottayam, the 1st to 17th accused formed an unlawful assembly. They carried sticks, sword-sticks and pestles/clubs. Knowing that Aanapappy’s death was likely, they deliberately beat and crushed his head and body with the sticks, struck him with the clubs and stabbed him with swords (Police FIR).

Under the heading “A Blood Offering for Aanapappy,” Paret recorded it this way: on Meenam 18, 1087, Varkey Varghese of Attupuram, Peringara, near Thiruvalla, a great wrestler worthy of the name Aanapappy, was offered up as a sacrifice on the sand-bank by the seminary.

At that time Fr. P. T. Geevarghese was deeply involved in community affairs. While preparing to leave the Malankara Church and join the Roman Church (he stated himself that it was not for the soul’s safety but for perfection), he wrote in Girideepam:

“Had such a murder not happened there, one wonders what the readers would now need to be considering. Terrible and unspeakable things could have happened there. Only because the yard of the Old Seminary drank human blood did nothing further happen. Everyone understood that this was the thirst the Syrian community had long had.”

Fr. P. T. Geevarghese, Girideepam

Yes, Aanapappy’s blood washed away the strife of that age. He was a giant of a man who, by shedding his own blood, ended the turmoil long visible in his community. The title “martyr” (rakthasakshi, literally “blood-witness”) fits him literally.

Dr. Dias, the medical officer who first saw Aanapappy’s extraordinary body after it was brought to the hospital, testified as the 21st witness in the case: “The deceased Varghese was a wrestler and an extraordinary strong man. In my service I have seen very few people with such a large, well-built body.”

In the Aanapappy case, the testimony of St. Dionysius of Vattasseril is also highly noteworthy:

“…I have been living permanently at the Kottayam Syrian Seminary for about four years. I know the dead Pappy. On the evil counsel of some people, certain persons pushed and stabbed Pappy and endangered him; that is what I know. It happened on the sand-bank north of the seminary. Pappy was from Thiruvalla. Pappy came to Kottayam for my protection; the Thiruvalla church people sent him. He first came toward the end of Kumbham. He stayed there 10–15 days and went to his house. Then Pappy came again on the day of the incident, after daybreak [five or six nazhika]. Besides Pappy, there were other people for my protection…”

St. Dionysius of Vattasseril, testimony in the Aanapappy case

On Wednesday, April 3, 1912, a report in Malayala Manorama told the world the details of the murder. It said that a man named Varkey, who lived with Mar Dionysius of Malankara and his people, was “last Saturday around five in the evening” beaten severely at the seminary landing by some Muslims and others and left near death, and that he died in hospital the next day. The report said the news could not fail to pain any human heart. When the people in the seminary had gone in for evening prayer, Varkey, thirsty, unarmed and unsuspecting, had gone to a nearby tea shop. The men lying in wait there ambushed him and beat him senseless.

You can imagine how cruel the act was. By the time he was brought to the hospital, Varkey’s story was practically over. The Assistant Surgeon immediately informed the Taluk First Class Magistrate, who came at once and recorded Varkey’s dying declaration. After death the Assistant Surgeon examined the body. The right leg’s heel tendon had been cut, and the skull had been smashed into eleven pieces. These findings were cited in the certificate as the cause of death.

On Monday the body was taken from the hospital. With the sympathy of the Kottayam Cheriyapally congregation and others, and an escort, it was buried in the Puthenpally cemetery, the cemetery of Kottayam Cheriyapally. The people of the parish showed their sympathy and helplessness. A memorial stone was later erected there in his name.

Undoubtedly, Varkey Varghese, called Aanapappy, remains an unforgettable wound in Malankara Church history. This young man gave his own life for a holy man and for his community, and the Church will forever sing praise of his sacrifice.

Attacked 10 September 1921 · Died 23 December 1921

Ponodathu Mathai Kathanar

പൊന്നോടൊത്ത് മത്തായി കത്തനാർ
Mulanthuruthy Marthoman ChurchVicar

Among the lay (almayakkar) martyrs, including Varkey Varghese (Aanapappy), who was killed while guarding St. Dionysius of Vattasseril, there are many. Among the clergy there is one: Ponodathu Mathai Kathanar (of Thoppil), vicar of Mulanthuruthy Marthoman Church.

He was born in 1872 into the Thoppil family, as the grandson of Cheriyathu Cheriya Kathanar. After primary schooling he began priestly studies as a disciple of Srambikkal Geevarghese Mar Gregorios of the Niranam Diocese (St. Gregorios of Parumala). St. Gregorios of Parumala took on this disciple, whom he had received from his own native region, with great affection and took responsibility for him. Soon after accepting him as a disciple, he gave him the Korooyo order, and St. Gregorios of Parumala himself gave all the orders up to Kasheesha (priest). After ordination he became vicar of his mother parish, Mulanthuruthy.

Out of love for this disciple, St. Gregorios of Parumala arranged his marriage to Aachi, a girl of the Vayaliparambil house of the Angamaly Akaparambu parish. He himself discussed and settled the marriage and the other arrangements. He gave the dowry that Mathai Kathanar received along with the rest from his own money, bought him a small plot and had a cope (kappa) stitched for him. Contemporaries described him as “capable in everything” and a truly respected clergyman. He had four sons and a daughter.

At that time Pulikkottil Joseph Mar Dionysius, the Malankara Metropolitan, felt special affection and trust for him, as did Vattasseril Geevarghese Mar Dionysius after him.

When St. Dionysius of Vattasseril refused to sign over the Malankara Church’s secular authority, Patriarch Abdulla II suspended him without cause on Edavam 26, 1911. He rejected the suspension as uncanonical. The Antiochian partisans who favoured it then made Kochuparambil Paulose Mar Koorilos of the Angamaly Diocese their leader and began agitating against St. Dionysius of Vattasseril and the Church. In the northern parishes this section had good influence, which fed bitter factionalism.

Mathai Kathanar took a firm stand everywhere and supported the canonical Metropolitan, St. Dionysius of Vattasseril. It posed no problem for him that Mar Paulose Koorilos, whom the opposition called the “Malankara Metropolitan,” was his own cousin (son of his father’s sister). He was able to keep the majority of the people of Mulanthuruthy with him, along with many members of other parishes.

Even as vicar, he served at Vettickal Dayara and at the chapel (a thatched hut of palm leaves) that St. Dionysius of Vattasseril set up where Carmel Dayara now stands. When he entrusted his turn at the wealthy Mulanthuruthy church to fellow priests, he still carried out services in these churches without lapse, unafraid.

The breakaway faction (later the Patriarchal faction) realized that taking Mulanthuruthy without eliminating Mathai Kathanar was impossible. They tried to suspend him through Paulose Athanasios (of Aluva), whom they regarded as the Malankara Metropolitan. It failed: three priests from the breakaway group in the Mulanthuruthy church also refused to go along with the suspension.

His death. On Chingam 25, 1097 (September 10, 1921), Mathai Kathanar and Chalil Korah were standing and talking on the second level of the church building (pallimeda) at Mulanthuruthy. A schoolteacher, frenzied with devotion to Antioch, ran up and struck them with a log of firewood, knocking both men down. Mathai Kathanar never recovered from the fall. After a few months he died on December 23, 1921 and was buried in the Mulanthuruthy church. The accused was held in a Kochi government jail, where he became deranged. After release he wandered about and died.

Meanwhile, St. Dionysius of Vattasseril and Kundara Kochu Metranachen (later Mar Baselios Geevarghese II Catholicos) came to Mulanthuruthy to visit him on his sickbed, and together performed the sick-anointing (kandeela) for the Achen. His sister’s grandson was the late Dr. Paulose Mar Gregorios, Metropolitan of Delhi.

Chalil Korah

ചാലിൽ കോര
Mulanthuruthy parishLay leader & social worker

Mathai Kathanar’s closest friend and helper was Chalil Korah. A social worker and popular leader, he stood firmly with the Achen at all times. A long-time member of the Malankara Church Managing Committee, he was a humane man in Mulanthuruthy church. The heads of the Chalil family held the hereditary lay leadership (manushyam) at Mulanthuruthy church.

Korah, whom one may call the sculptor of modern Mulanthuruthy, was the son of Chalil Kunjicheria and Konat Unnichar. Mulanthuruthy St. Thomas School, the first English school of the Malankara Church in the northern region, was founded in 1887 through his efforts, and handed to the government in 1914. The government hospital, registrar’s office, post office and girls’ school in Mulanthuruthy, the Chottanikkara–Piravom road and the restoration of the church were all his work. When the Mulanthuruthy Panchayat was formed, its first president was Chalil Korah. Twice the Maharaja of Cochin gave him the Keerthimudra (honour) and he sat in the royal durbar. After his father’s death in 1891 he took over the church’s lay leadership, and he was among the popular leaders who stood with St. Dionysius of Vattasseril.

Mar Alvares and the Relief Fund

Antonio Francisco Xavier Alvares, Mar Julius, was Metropolitan of the Indian, Goan and Ceylon parishes. A longstanding bond existed between him and Chalil Korah, as church historian Dr. M. Kurian Thomas notes.

From the start, the Mar Alvares Mission struggled for manpower and financial security. He constantly begged the Malankara Church for priests and money. For social and linguistic reasons, only temporary priestly service was possible, and the Church, bound by custom, could not give much financial help. Even so, Pulikkottil Joseph Mar Dionysius V and Vattasseril Geevarghese Mar Dionysius VI helped him financially through many parish churches.

St. Gregorios of Parumala confirms this. In a letter of April 7, 1899, to Patriarch Ignatius Abdul Masih II, he wrote:

“…Abun Julius Alvaris is now in Colombo… his and his disciples’ work is mostly among Catholics, but a great deal of money is needed. Because of the lack of money he does not act as he would like. He and his disciples constantly ask us for money. From where shall we give them? We need money more than he does. But from our own poverty we are helping them considerably to establish them…”

St. Gregorios of Parumala, letter of 7 April 1899

After Abdulla II’s illegitimate suspension of St. Dionysius of Vattasseril, Mar Alvares declared his support for him through a public decree on June 15, 1911. Dr. Kurian Thomas thinks it likely that Chalil Korah, active in community affairs, would have come to know Mar Alvares then.

False rumours spread by Roman Catholics claimed Mar Alvares had died (1921). On Sunday, May 29, 1921, a meeting of the Mulanthuruthy parish was held at Chalil Korah’s church. It was told that the rumour of Mar Alvares’s death was false, that his present condition was pitiable, and that his good deeds for the Syrian Church must not be forgotten. Resolutions:

  • begin efforts to help Mar Alvares immediately;
  • start a Mar Alvares Mission Relief Fund collection;
  • form local committees in other parishes;
  • once enough money was raised, send a deacon or priest to Goa to build a chapel and house for Mar Alvares.

A ten-member committee was formed, with Chalil Korah as president and Chathuruthil Mathai Varkey as secretary and treasurer.

A detailed report appeared in the July 1921 issue of the Catholic Sabha magazine and magazine workers fully backed it, but the Relief Fund never materialized. Within two months of that report, Chalil Korah’s martyrdom left the effort leaderless. No help like the Mulanthuruthy congregation’s ever came from elsewhere. As a result, the independent Catholic movement of Mar Alvares, once spread across South India and Sri Lanka, has shrunk to just the Konkani community in the Brahmavar diocese.

His death. On Chingam 25, 1097 (September 10, 1921), while Mathai Kathanar and Chalil Korah were talking on the second level of the church building, they were attacked as described above. Chalil Korah died two days later, on Chingam 27 (September 12, 1921).

No photograph is known to survive

Martyred 27 Chingam 1103 M.E. (12 September 1927)

Cheppad Puthenveettil Kochunnunni

ചേപ്പാട്ട് കൊച്ചുണ്ണൂണ്ണി
Cheppad church

As part of the Antiochian Patriarch’s scheme, the Patriarchal faction installed Ottaithaikkal Thomas Kathanar, under the name “Dioscoros,” in place of the late Mar Severios of the Knanaya community. This came from a desire to dim the radiance of St. Dionysius of Vattasseril. In September 1927, Elias III also consecrated Michael Kathanar, son of John Upadeshi of Alummoottil, Kayamkulam, as a rival “Dionysius.”

This caused many problems in Church affairs. The “rival” Metropolitan tried to oppose St. Dionysius of Vattasseril and to seize the community’s properties and manpower, but produced nothing except factionalism and rivalry. Using his influence from Kottayam to the south, Michael Dionysius tried to enter many churches forcefully and hold services, sometimes causing rough clashes.

On Chingam 27, 1103 (September 12, 1927), Kochunnunni of Cheppad Puthenveettil became a martyr in the clash that occurred when Michael Metropolitan entered Cheppad church. He was buried in the Cheppad church cemetery.

Three accounts of this incident are recorded in Paret’s Malankara Nasranikal, Vol. 4:

  • One says Michael Metran went to Cheppad church, the factions clashed at his entry, and he struck the Cheppad Metran’s sister’s grandson (the brother of Philipose Ramban) with his hand. Others then attacked with weapons, and within two or three days a man died.
  • The diary entry of St. Dionysius of Vattasseril (Chingam 29, 1103, written at Thiruvananthapuram) says: “Last Saturday the 25th, in the evening, Michael Metran secretly entered Cheppad church and a brawl broke out. The Metran’s stick broke into three pieces and he ran away. One man caught in the brawl was struck and died.”
  • Fr. Paret’s (Mar Ivanios’s) diary entry, written at Bethany, Thiruvalla, in a note of Chingam 30, says the Metran struck the Achen and the Achen struck the Metran, the Metran’s silver staff broke, and “during the clash, Achen’s brother’s son was beaten and died.” This was reported by Mavelikkara Mathai Panicker (Mar Ivanios’s younger brother).

Together, these leave no doubt the event is true. It is also covered by name and detail in Cheppad Philipose Ramban’s Poorvakala Smaranakal (“Reminiscences”), which relates:

“…After supper at home, while chatting, I heard the church bell ringing hard. Shortly after, the sacristan came running: ‘Michael Metran Achen has come into the church; Pulimoottil Thomas, Evarathu Kizhakkethil Mathunni, Kannimala Achen and Thayyil Achen are all there.’ I went to the church at once. Both factions had come running at the bell. Seeing me, my side came and asked what was needed. ‘We can’t just stand watching. The Metran Achen did not enter the church peacefully; it must be made public,’ they said, and rushed into the church.

The one who went and stood at the head was a deacon (shemmasan), raising the Thirumeni’s amsavadi (ceremonial staff). They told him, ‘You are not the one who should hold the amsavadi in this church,’ and snatched it away. When the other faction came and grabbed at it too, the staff broke into pieces in the tussle. Michael Metran Achen, frightened, fled into the madbaha (altar). The Thayyil Achen who was inside saw this and ran out through the madbaha’s north door. He climbed onto a prop tied against the back of the wall, lost his footing, fell on his back and slid down. Somehow he got up and fled; that is how frightened he was.

Then three policemen arrived and put everyone out of the church. The police took the staff’s pieces; the golden cross set between the staff’s two serpent heads and the other parts were never recovered. They kept the Thirumeni seated in the church, put everyone else outside and shut the doors. One policeman stood guard, and the other two began writing the mahazar. People crowded around, and I went home. On Sunday, Chingam 26, 1103, Michael Metran Achen celebrated Qurbana at Cheppad church. By then a Magistrate’s order to leave the church immediately came, and he left. News of his entry into Cheppad church and the scattering of the staff spread in all four directions like wildfire.”

Cheppad Philipose Ramban, Poorvakala Smaranakal

Kochunnunni’s death at Puthenveettil. St. Dionysius of Vattasseril was then in Thiruvananthapuram. Philipose Ramban’s account continues:

“…Thinking he should be told every detail, I went to Thiruvananthapuram and asked. My brother-in-law, Muthakunju of Alummoottil, was also there. A telegram came while we were in Thiruvananthapuram: ‘My brother, Kochunnunni of Cheppad Puthenveettil, has died.’ He had been taking a milk-based tonic, and died of a blow in the scuffle in the church. That was Chingam 27, 1103. We returned to Cheppad at once and held Kochunnunni’s funeral.

In the ‘stick-clash’ in the church, one man who had gripped Kochunnunni hard from the opposite side was also in the crowd that came running when the news of the death spread. Kochunnunni’s dog lay silently in the yard. That dog got up, went through the crowd, gave the man a good bite on the leg, tore at it with a growl and ran away. Everyone was astonished. The dog never came back home; it left the house and went off somewhere.”

Cheppad Philipose Ramban, Poorvakala Smaranakal

Factional rivalry has caused martyr deaths over many generations, and innocents like Kochunnunni are often its victims.

11 August 1951 – 15 February 1976

Kadamattom Onankunju

കടമറ്റം ഓനൻകുഞ്ഞ്
Kadamattom parish

The 1970s were the most intense phase of the Malankara Church’s second round of division. On February 16, 1975, at Niranam church, five bishops were consecrated on the Metropolitan side. In August–September that year, Baselios Augen I vacated the offices of Malankara Metropolitan and Catholicos, Mathews I was enthroned as Catholicos, and Augen I died on December 8.

The sudden emergence of new leadership was a heavy blow to the breakaway faction. Already the unauthorized Metropolitan positions taken by Perumbilly Fr. Geevarghese and Cheruvally Fr. Thomas had paved the way for deep upheaval and factionalism in the northern region of the Church. It is true that many were led astray by the skill of these two, who were adept at any drama needed to keep people with them. The ceaseless efforts of such figures to sustain themselves, along with factional hatred, led to harsh disputes in churches.

At this point, the newly consecrated Mar Baselios Marthoma Mathews I and the new Metropolitans were given the most regal reception Kerala had seen, on February 15, 1976, in Ernakulam. The report in that month’s Malankara Sabha magazine said it was the most heartfelt and exciting reception ever given on a visit to Ernakulam, on Sunday evening, February 15, 1976. Ernakulam, adorned with arches and banners, became a sea of people. Accompanied by hundreds of cars from MD Seminary, Kottayam, the Catholicos and Metropolitans Paulose Mar Gregorios, Geevarghese Mar Osthathios, Stephanos Mar Theodosios and Joseph Mar Pakkomios came in procession via Ettumanoor, Koothattukulam, Muvattupuzha, Kolenchery and Thripunithura and reached the city outskirts at 3:30 pm.

Veekshanam (mouthpiece of Kerala’s then-ruling Congress) and Malayala Manorama carried reports:

“Thousands of faithful who arrived by vehicle from various parts of Malankara showed their support for the Church’s strong leadership and faith.”

Veekshanam

“Today’s reception procession in Ernakulam spread the light of spiritual vitality. This ancient city has not seen so large a spiritual rally in recent times, and it was entirely under the lead of the northern dioceses. Like waves rolling in off the Arabian sea, lakhs of people poured through all of Ernakulam’s roads. No other reception with so many motor vehicles has ever visited Ernakulam.”

Malayala Manorama

This reception, held under the lead of the northern dioceses, shook even the then-ruling establishment. It was not like the paid-publicity “praise-and-glorification” events staged today by the Puthencruz group (the Jacobite faction, whose headquarters is at Puthencruz). It dealt an irreparable blow to the breakaway faction, and the resulting enmity paved the way for the fierce murder that followed. To take part in the great convention, believers left Kadamattom parish in three big lorries.

When the vehicle crossed Puthenkurishu and neared the Varikkoli church, the breakaway faction’s goons waved black flags at it, shouted slogans and threatened murder. They tried to stop the lorry but failed, and the Kadamattom people joyfully took part in the convention.

After the convention, because of the threat, two of the three lorries returned from Mamala via Thiruvaniyoor and Pattimattom. One lorry took the same route, hoping that, with the delay, there would be no trouble. On it was Onankunju, also named Kuriako Yuhanon, of the Karavattemangalam family in Kadamattom parish.

His life. Born August 11, 1951, in Ramamangalam Panchayat, Muvattupuzha Taluk, Ernakulam district, Onankunju was the youngest of the seven children of Kuriako and Annamma. He studied up to seventh class at Ooramana Government High School, looked after family matters and was deeply involved in spiritual life. Unmarried, he was very eager in Church affairs. At the great convention he took part with enthusiasm, calling out welcome-greetings to the Fathers.

His death. The lorry was fitted with bamboo poles tied into a pandal-like frame, and the sides were open. Tired, Onankunju was dozing a little on the return trip, holding on with his legs at the lorry’s rear. As the lorry went down the slope through Mamala and Sasthamukal, bricks and stone pieces suddenly came flying at it. A brick hit Onankunju’s head as he leaned on the rail, and he fell backward onto the road. He died instantly from the blow and the fall.

The lorry went a little further. When fellow travellers ran back, they saw him laid on the ledge of a roadside house. The goons had vanished. He was taken first to Kolenchery hospital, then to Ernakulam Medical College, where his death was confirmed. The eyewitness and fellow traveller Mr. George Poonthottil, later also a witness in the case, described this vividly to the writer. On the night of February 15, 1976, Onankunju, only 24, attained martyrdom for the Holy Church.

His funeral was conducted with the Church’s full honours at St. George Orthodox Syrian Church cemetery, Kadamattom, attended by Holy Bava Thirumeni (Mathews I), Geevarghese Mar Osthathios, Joseph Mar Pakkomios and other Metropolitans, many priests and a huge crowd. Bava Thirumeni gave the family a large sum for those days, and many others also helped. A protest convention was held at Kadamattom Pallithazham against this cruelty and the Patriarchal faction’s anti-Orthodox activities. On March 9, 1976, at the Malankara Orthodox Church Managing Committee, Fr. Joseph Vendrappilly moved a condolence resolution on Onankunju, which the committee passed. A memorial was later built in the cemetery.

9 April 1938 – 31 August 1993

Thottappattu Kuriakose

തോട്ടപ്പാട്ട് കുര്യാക്കോസ്
Perumbavoor Bethel Suloko parishCar driver

Thottappattu Kuriakose, a member of the Perumbavoor Bethel Suloko parish, was from an ordinary family. He was born on April 9, 1938, to Thottappattu Uthup and Annamma. He supported his wife and five children by working as a car driver. His wife was Leelamma of the Puthenpurayil family of South Vazhakkulam in Aluva. Their children are Elias, Shajan Joshy, Baiju, Bindu and Sindhu.

Despite hardship, he was a model churchgoer who loved his parish and stood for the Church. He was the first martyr from Suloko parish, and Malankara Varghese was the second. Their friendship went beyond neighbourliness, and the Patriarchal faction disliked it. Realizing that the Patriarchal faction could not survive at Suloko unless these two were destroyed, they sought many ways to eliminate both.

The Patriarchal side routinely created problems and harassed parish members, including the Orthodox. The then-vicar of the opposing party, Pathikkal Ouseph Kathanar, who led the divisive activities, issued a notice excommunicating seven Orthodox families and had it distributed to houses. The notice said no one should keep any relation with the excommunicated or their houses, or attend any ceremony there. Malankara Varghese and Thottappattu Kuriakose led the protest.

On most Sundays the opposing side insulted the Orthodox members of the parish, used indecent language and tormented them mentally. Pushing, shoving and shouted challenges were all used to win their side. There was no parish office then, and the Orthodox side had a temporary office outside the church. By a Paravur Munsiff court order, the Orthodox side could celebrate Holy Qurbana in the church on Sunday mornings from 6 to 8:45.

In this tense setting came the wedding of Pathikkal Ouseph Kathanar’s son. Under the existing court order, only the vicars of the two sides could enter the church, and no other priests or bishops. News came that Thomas Mar Dionysius, who then held charge of the Angamaly Diocese, would attend. To protest, Orthodox members and the church president left their temporary office and went to the church door. They told the priests there to follow the court order, or the Orthodox would protest. They held a peaceful sit-in with slogans.

Meanwhile a whistle sounded, and ruffians (kapalikas) from the Patriarchal section, who had been waiting in the shed, attacked the Orthodox with stones, cudgels and other weapons. Malankara Varghese, a marked target, was felled by a thrown stone, and Kuriakose was struck on the head with a cudgel. Severely wounded and unconscious, Kuriakose was taken to Ernakulam Medical Trust Hospital. Despite treatment, he died on the twelfth day. The sole support of his wife and five children, he left the family facing an uncertain future.

On August 31, 1993, when he departed, he was only 55. He was buried at Perumbavoor Bethel Suloko church. Catholicos-designate Thomas Mar Timotheos Thirumeni (Didymos I) and other bishops conducted the funeral.

13 January 1946 – 5 December 2002

Malankara Varghese

മലങ്കര വർഗീസ്
Perumbavoor Bethel Suloko parishTimber merchant

T. M. Varghese was born on January 13, 1946, as the third son of Mathai and Annamma of the respected Thombra family in Perumbavoor. When he was five his father, a leading farmer, died, and his mother raised him. Though he lost his father’s love and care, his mother brought him up well and his childhood was happy. T. M. Mathai and T. M. Jacob were his brothers, and his sister Eli Saramma died early.

He studied at Perumbavoor Government Boys’ High School and passed SSLC in 1962, but could not continue because of family circumstances. That same year he joined a sawmill as a clerk. From then on he was devoted to church matters, serving as a church helper and studying the Bible and Syriac. His distinctive devotion and ideals made him popular with colleagues.

After nine years at the sawmill, he began a business with a partner in 1971. He rose quickly in the timber trade and was noted for honesty and spirituality. In 1979 he began his own business and that year was elected president of the Kerala Sawmill Owners’ Association, holding the post for 13 years, longer than anyone. His firm, Malankara Timber Industries, is why he came to be called “Malankara Varghese.” His deep love for the Church led him to adopt that name. He never neglected church matters. He was active in the youth movement and Sunday school, joined the Perumbavoor church leadership, opposed alcohol and was a strict vegetarian. His pure nature and firm faith made him well accepted among the congregation.

In 1974 he married Saramma, eldest daughter of Poulose and Mariamma of the Azhakam Maliyekkal family. They had four children: Tilson, Tinsy, Tinmy and Nucy.

Representing the Angamaly Diocese, he was first elected to the Church Managing Committee in 1982 and held the post until his death. In that turbulent period he led efforts to resolve the issues of the Perumbavoor Church and the Angamaly Diocese. He was an evangelist interested in training youth in church matters, and served as Sunday School teacher and inspector and Diocesan Council representative. In 1986 he became a teacher in the Divine Instruction (Divyabodhanam) programme. In 1987 he received the Trans World Trade Fair Selection Award from the President for the honesty he showed in business. He also took part in temperance activities.

Active in the Church dispute, he sent a registered notice on October 29, 1986, to Thomas Mar Dionysius, the breakaway Angamaly Metropolitan, saying the self-proclaimed “Sreshta Bava (Catholicos)” must not enter the diocese and would be stopped if he tried. He then filed a case against an office-holder not recognised by law entering Perumbavoor Bethel Suloko church and obtained an injunction. This became the reason for his murder.

On December 5, 2002, in broad daylight, Malankara Varghese was deliberately killed by the Patriarchal section, reportedly in retribution for the court verdicts against them. Press reports (and the FIR) say Rs 60,000 was paid to those hired for the killing, with large sums also spent renting a house to lodge the goons, feeding them and running the car used. That some prominent members of the breakaway side paid this was no secret.

First Information Report (No. 701)

Submitted to Perumbavoor JFCM-I on 5.12.2002. The murder occurred about 1:30 pm on 5.12.2002 at Karottappady Junction, about one and a half km south of Perumbavoor police station. Rajan, 34, son of Karan, of Kombanad Villan Paramudikarayil Puthenveettil, appeared at the station at 2:10 pm and told Sub-Inspector K. N. Muraleedharan:

“I run an engineering workshop at Karottappady Junction, Perumbavoor. A man named Velayudhan runs an automobile workshop in the room just south of mine. Today (5.12.2002) around 1:30 pm, in front of Velayudhan’s workshop by the road, a car with 3–4 people stopped. One of them opened the right rear door and, holding a sword, ran to where Varghese Chettan was standing. I saw him surround Varghese Chettan and strike him 3–4 times. Seeing this, I and Sanjoy, the workshop hand who was repairing a car, ran north. We went along the back of the workshop, and when we came back to the front we saw people there putting Varghese Chettan into a Maruti car. His legs had been hacked and were dangling. The attacker and those in the car then got in and sped north.

Today Varghese Chettan’s car had been brought to the workshop to fit the rear bumper. Velayudhan Chettan had gone to lunch, and Varghese Chettan was waiting for him; at that time one of those who came in the car hacked him with a sword to kill. They came in a white Ambassador; I didn’t notice its number. We ran in fear on seeing the sword. The attacker wore a white mundu and white shirt and was about 40. I would recognize him and the sword if I saw them again. There was a lot of blood where Varghese Chettan was struck. I know him well; he would be over 59. The scene of the incident is half a km from here, to the south. I don’t know the reason for the incident. I studied up to 5th–6th class.”

Rajan, eyewitness statement recorded in FIR No. 701, 5 December 2002

When the 1995 court verdicts and changing circumstances threatened the survival of the breakaway side, history repeated itself through this incident. History bears witness that these people have no qualms about killing those who stand for the Church’s truth and justice. The accused and those charged in the case, including the self-proclaimed “Sreshta Bava (Catholicos)” and the self-surrendered Thekkekkara Kathanar and accomplices, faced legal action; high-level investigation teams and the CBI took up the case.

Catholicos-designate Thomas Mar Timotheos, Niranam Diocese Metropolitan Dr. Geevarghese Mar Osthathios and other Metropolitans officiated at the funeral at Perumbavoor Bethel Suloko Church, before thousands of believers. The Catholicos cut short a foreign tour and returned to console the family. T. M. Varghese was a powerful speaker of the Malankara Orthodox Church and loved the Church even more than his own family. No one who knew him doubts this. Malankara Varghese still lives in the minds of believers.

No Malankara Nasrani has ever consented, or will ever consent, to surrender the Malankara Church’s independence and self-awareness or Mar Thoma’s paternal heritage, or to give up the Church’s traditions out of personal interest or temptation. The lives of these martyrs, who sacrificed themselves against Antiochian slavery, still cry out against those who strive to bind Mother Church in bondage. The Church’s dealings carry centuries of history, and the losses and bloodshed of those times march on in the Church as unhealed wounds and sleepless memories.

Aanapappy, Ponodathu Mathai Kathanar, Chalil Korah, Cheppad Kochunnunni, Kadamattom Onankunju, Thottappattu Kuriakose, and Malankara Varghese remain the steadfast, courageous martyrs of the Malankara Church. This Church and its people will never forget the blood and tears they shed.

Before the brave memory of the martyrs of Malankara, we offer a thousand flowers of tears.
