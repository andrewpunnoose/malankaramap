In Remembrance

Martyrs of Malankara

Behind the history of the Malankara Church’s self-governance and independence lies an unforgettable history of the courageous struggles and sufferings of its faithful. The heroic history of the Nasrani community of Malankara — who fought and persevered, relying solely on the faith, traditions, and heritage of St. Thomas, which they had received through Christ, against the Roman and Syrian forces that attempted to subjugate them by presenting submission to ecclesiastical authority as a virtue and placing the shackles of slavery upon them — remains relevant even today.

Throughout the history of the Church, there have been numerous martyrs who, because they stood with the Holy Church and refused to surrender the Church’s independence and principles, were subjected to deliberate and indiscriminate killings. There were also many who were attacked and isolated alongside them. Even today, these attacks continue in one form or another.

We do not forget that there were also others whose names remain unknown.

The seven faithful remembered on this page were murdered for their faith by Antiochian schismatics, across more than a century of conflict in the northern regions of Malankara.

It remains a matter of historical record that, throughout this long conflict, no member of the Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church has been found to have attacked or taken the life of a member of the schismatic faction — a restraint that stands in solemn contrast to the repeated violence her own faithful have suffered.

A candle has been lit in memory of the martyrs.

Martyred 5 December 2002

Malankara Varghese

മലങ്കര വർഗീസ്
Perumbavoor Bethel Suloko Orthodox Cathedral Timber merchant & lay preacher

T. M. Varghese, remembered as Malankara Varghese, was a member of the Managing Committee of the Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church. A timber merchant, lay preacher, and steadfast defender of the Church, he named his business Malankara Timbers as an expression of his devotion to her.

On 2 December 2002, the Kerala High Court delivered its judgment concerning the Kochi, Kandanad East, Kandanad West, and Angamaly dioceses, affirming the legal standing of parish administration under the Church’s 1934 Constitution. In the days that followed, Varghese travelled throughout the Angamaly Diocese, publicly explaining and defending the judgment and the authority of the Catholicos. On 5 December 2002, only three days later, he was brutally murdered by Antiochian schismatic assailants near Perumbavoor.

His assassination is understood by his family to have been ordered by Baselios Thomas I, leader of the Patriarchal faction, with his personal secretary, Fr. Varghese Thekkekara, entrusted with planning the killing — on account of Varghese’s prominent role in defending the Orthodox Church and her legal rights.

“If my death can pave the way for the Malankara Church to be transformed into one free of conflict, I will accept it courageously.”

Malankara Varghese, at Kunnakkurudy St. George Orthodox Cathedral, shortly before his death
9 April 1938 – 31 August 1993

Thottappattu Uthup Kuriakose

തോട്ടപ്പാട്ട് കുര്യാക്കോസ്
Perumbavoor Bethel Suloko Orthodox Cathedral

Born on 9 April 1938, Thottappattu Uthup Kuriakose was a faithful member of Perumbavoor Bethel Suloko Orthodox Cathedral. On 31 August 1993, he was beaten to death at the Cathedral, struck about the head with wooden sticks by Antiochian schismatic assailants — a killing that arose from the same factional conflict over the Church’s independence that has claimed so many of her faithful.

11 August 1951 – 15 February 1976

Kadamattom Onankunju

കടമറ്റം ഓനൻകുഞ്ഞ്
Kadamattom St. George Orthodox Church

Kuriako Yohannan, known to his parish as Onankunju, was born on 11 August 1951 at the Karavattu Mangalathu house in Ramamangalam Panchayat, Muvattupuzha Taluk, the youngest of the seven children of Kuriako and Annamma. Educated to the seventh standard at the Government High School, Ooramana, he remained unmarried and devoted himself to the affairs of his family and of the Church.

On 15 February 1976, Onankunju travelled with fellow parishioners of Kadamattom, in a convoy of three lorries, to Ernakulam to take part in the historic reception held for the newly enthroned Catholicos, His Holiness Baselios Marthoma Mathews I, and the four newly consecrated Metropolitans. Returning that night, as their lorry passed near Puthenkurishu and Mattakuzhy, members of the Patriarchal faction — who had earlier threatened the convoy with black flags and cries of menace — pelted the vehicle with bricks and stones. Struck in the head, Onankunju fell from the lorry to the road and died instantly, at the age of twenty-five.

He was buried with the full honours of the Church at the cemetery of Kadamattom St. George Orthodox Church, in the presence of Geevarghese Mar Osthathios and Joseph Mar Pachomios — representing His Holiness the Catholicos — together with numerous clergy and a vast gathering of the faithful. His death provoked such public outcry that the then Chief Minister of Kerala, C. Achutha Menon, publicly called upon both factions of the Malankara Church to end their costly and provocative demonstrations.

No photograph is known to survive

Martyrdom date to be recorded

Cheppattu Kochunnunni

ചേപ്പാട്ട് കൊച്ചുണ്ണൂണ്ണി
Cheppad St. George Orthodox Church

Kochunnunni of Cheppad St. George Orthodox Church was a lay organizer and liaison of long standing within the Malankara Church. He is remembered in particular for his role in the Church’s arrangements for the October 1956 state visit of His Imperial Majesty Haile Selassie I, Emperor of Ethiopia, to Malankara. Working closely with the leadership of the Church, the Manager of the Old Seminary, its priests, and government officials, he helped coordinate the Emperor’s itinerary, the ceremonial arrangements, the preparations for his reception, and the presentation of gifts.

The circumstances of his martyrdom are not yet fully documented on this page, and will be added as further sources come to light.

No photograph is known to survive

Martyred December 1921

Chalil Korah

ചാലിൽ കോര
Mulanthuruthy St. Thomas Orthodox Cathedral Trustee & social reformer

Chalil Korah was trustee of Mulanthuruthy St. Thomas Orthodox Cathedral and a member of the Malankara Church Managing Committee, remembered also as a social reformer instrumental in the development of modern Mulanthuruthy. In 1902, during the reorganization of the Malankara Church into dioceses, he served as one of four administrators of Vettickal Dayara, overseeing its affairs under the authority of the Patriarch alongside Kadavil Mor Athanasius Metropolitan and the other appointed administrators.

In December 1921, amid the deepening factional dispute between the Catholicos and Patriarchal parties, Korah was set upon without provocation in the church parsonage at Mulanthuruthy, together with the parish vicar, Ponodathu Mathai Kathanar. He died instantly of his injuries.

Died 23 December 1921

Ponodathu Mathai Kathanar

പൊനോടത്ത് മത്തായി കത്തനാർ
Mulanthuruthy St. Thomas Orthodox Cathedral Vicar

Ponodathu Mathai Kathanar, vicar of Mulanthuruthy St. Thomas Orthodox Cathedral and grandson of Fr. Thoppil Cheriyathu Kathanar — who had also served the parish as vicar — held office in the years surrounding the historic Mulanthuruthy Synod of 1876. In his native place he was remembered as the sole disciple of St. Gregorios of Parumala.

In December 1921, amid the bitter dispute between the Catholicos and Patriarchal factions, Mathai Kathanar was attacked without provocation in the church parsonage, together with the parish trustee, Chalil Korah, who died instantly. Rendered unconscious by the assault, Mathai Kathanar succumbed to his injuries on 23 December 1921 and was buried the following day at Mulanthuruthy St. Thomas Orthodox Cathedral — the only priest to have given his life in this long conflict. His descendants include Fr. Dr. Jossi Jacob, Principal of the Orthodox Seminary at Nagpur, and Ebin Mathai, trustee of Mulanthuruthy St. Thomas Orthodox Cathedral.

Martyred 31 March 1912

Varkey Varghese Aashan (Aanapappy)

വർക്കി ആശാൻ (ആനപാപ്പി)
Karackal St. George Orthodox Church, Peringara Schoolteacher & bodyguard to St. Dionysius

Varkey Varghese, affectionately known as Aanapappy, was a schoolteacher of uncommon stature and strength, trained in traditional martial arts and deeply devoted to the Malankara Church. Around the turn of 1912, as the schismatic Patriarchal faction escalated its threats against Vattasseril Geevarghese Mar Dionysius — the Great Luminary of the Malankara Church, then travelling the parishes of Malankara to proclaim the truth of the Church’s autocephaly against the claims of the Patriarch — the elders of Thiruvalla parish appointed Aanapappy chief among the bodyguards charged with St. Dionysius’s protection.

Unable to defeat the Metropolitan’s bodyguard by open confrontation, his enemies resolved that Aanapappy himself must first be removed. Schismatic goons hid their weapons in the sand at the riverside bathing place he frequented near the Old Seminary at Kottayam, and on 31 March 1912 fell upon him there, hacking him to death.

Though Aanapappy was martyred, the conspirators were never able to carry out their design against St. Dionysius himself — and it is in no small part to Aanapappy’s sacrifice that the Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church owes the survival of her autocephaly to this day. He was buried at the Cheriapally cemetery in Kottayam, where a memorial stone in his honour has since been raised.
