History

The first Malankara Orthodox priest in the United Kingdom was Fr. Dr. Shapurji Dadabhai Bhabha, ordained in Jerusalem in 1903 and made the Church's ambassador to the UK. A convert from Zoroastrianism, he joined the Church on learning of the ancient Church of St. Thomas. Later, Abo Alexios of the Bethany Monastery (later Bishop Mar Theodosius) became the first priest of the ancient Indian Church to visit the United Kingdom under an educational fellowship.

A gradual influx of Malayali Christian immigrants led to sporadic services in London and, in 1976, the formal establishment of a parish under the patronage of St. Gregorios of Parumala. In 1979 the community passed from the Diocese of Delhi and, in 1993, to the newly formed Diocese of Europe, UK and Canada under Thomas Mar Makarios. In May 2009, in view of rapid growth across multiple continents, the Holy Episcopal Synod divided the territory to establish the Diocese of UK, Europe and Africa. It was led initially by H.E. Dr. Mathews Mar Thimothios and has been overseen by H.E. Abraham Mar Stephanos since November 2022. Headquartered at St. Gregorios Indian Orthodox Cathedral, Brockley, London, it spans the UK, mainland Europe and Africa.
