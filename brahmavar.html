Diocese of Brahmavar

Formed in 2010 and administered from Mangalore, the Diocese of Brahmavar is the smallest in the Church and is unique for its Konkani Orthodox community and multilingual character.

History

The Diocese of Brahmavar was formed in August 2010. It was announced by Baselios Marthoma Didymus I through Kalpana No. 389/2010 of 3 August 2010, with the counsel of the Malankara Syrian Christian Association Managing Committee and on the recommendation of the Holy Episcopal Synod, both of which met on the same date. On 4 August 2010, by Kalpana No. 396/2010, H.G. Yakob Mar Elias was appointed Metropolitan of the new diocese, with headquarters at Mangalore.

The diocese is the smallest in size and the most modest in financial resources, and has grown through the support of its clergy and faithful, notably the parishioners of St. George Orthodox Cathedral, Abu Dhabi. Its territory comprises Kasaragod and parts of Kannur district in Kerala; Dakshina Kannada, Udupi, Chikmagalur and Coorg in Karnataka; the state of Goa; and Abu Dhabi overseas. It is remarkable for its diversity of culture, language and region, with Malayalam, Konkani, Kannada, Tulu, English and Hindi in use.

The Konkani Orthodox community, the only non-Malayali community in the Church, has been part of the Malankara Orthodox Church for some 125 years, owing to the vision and mission of two Roman Catholic priests who accepted the Church and its faith. The diocese is served by Konkani priests. Its centres are Mangalore as administrative centre, Brahmavar as spiritual and monastic centre, and Goa as pilgrim centre. The principal pilgrim sites are St. Mary's Orthodox Church, Panaji, Goa, where St. Alvares Mar Julius I is entombed, and St. George Orthodox Church, the Georgian Pilgrim Centre at Ichilampady.
